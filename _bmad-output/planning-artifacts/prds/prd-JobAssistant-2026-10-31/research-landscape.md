# Research landscape for the JobAssistant PRD

Research date: 2026-10-31. Method: web search plus page fetches; Norwegian, Swedish and Danish sources are translated into English. Areas: A comparable products, B ATS reality in Norway, C keyword matching in Norwegian, D privacy and GDPR for CVs sent to third-party LLM APIs. This is research input, not legal advice.

**Tags used**

- `UNVERIFIED`: could not be confirmed from any source I could read.

## A. Comparable products

### A1. International tools

**Jobscan** (the reference for a "match rate")
- Positioning: compares a CV with one job posting and returns a "match rate"; says it "is designed to mimic the behaviors of the most popular ATS". Reaches students through university career services (WGU gives students Premium). https://www.jobscan.co/jobscan-tutorial ; https://careers.wgu.edu/resources/jobscan-information-for-faculty/
- Score: 0-100% keyword match. Stated priority order: hard skills, education (only if the ad requires an advanced degree), job title, soft skills, other keywords. No formula or weights published. Target "at least 75%" (tutorial) or 80% (blog). https://www.jobscan.co/jobscan-tutorial ; https://www.jobscan.co/blog/what-jobscan-match-rate-should-i-aim-for/
- Limits: matching relies on exact keywords ("managed projects" may not count as "project management"); WGU's page does not say how the algorithm was validated. https://careers.wgu.edu/resources/jobscan-information-for-faculty/
- Free vs paid: limited free scans; Premium $49.95/month or $89.95/quarter per Jobscan's own comparison post (vendor-authored; its pricing page did not render). https://jobscan.co/blog/jobscan-vs-teal/
- Honesty: warns against cramming keywords "even when it's no longer truthful" and against listing skills you are not proficient in; WGU says a 100% match is unnecessary and AI letters are "a starting point". The same blog asserts "70% of resumes get rejected by the ATS before they get reviewed by humans" with no source. https://www.jobscan.co/blog/what-jobscan-match-rate-should-i-aim-for/

**Resume Worded**
- Positioning: free "Score My Resume", paid line-by-line feedback and rewriting; says scoring adapts to career level ("what counts as a strong resume for a student is different from ... a senior executive"). https://resumeworded.com/upload-resume
- Score: overall 0-100 from 20+ weighted checks in Impact, Brevity and Style; a separate "Relevancy Score" (Targeted Resume tool) measures fit to a job description and weights required qualifications above preferred ones; above 80 is "well matched". Weights not disclosed. https://resumeworded.com/score-guide ; https://help.resumeworded.com/article/33-how-is-my-score-calculated
- Price: paid-tier prices UNVERIFIED (pricing page 404; third-party reviews disagree).
- Honesty: the pages read say nothing about fabricated claims or keyword stuffing; search summaries say its advice is to place keywords inside experience bullets with evidence `[snippet]`. https://resumeworded.com/resume-keyword-scanner

**Teal** (first-party pages blocked; mostly `[snippet]`)
- Positioning: job tracker + resume builder + "Match Score" against a saved job.
- Score: keyword comparison; each keyword shown green (in resume), yellow (only in master resume) or red (absent); 80%+ treated as good; Teal says it is not an ATS score `[snippet]`. https://help.tealhq.com/en/articles/14435726-how-to-tailor-your-resume-for-a-specific-job ; https://www.tealhq.com/tool/resume-job-description-match
- Free vs paid: free plan has limited AI and shows only the top keywords `[snippet]` https://www.tealhq.com/pricing . Jobscan's comparison post lists Teal+ at $13/week, $29/month, $79/quarter (vendor-authored by a competitor; other sources differ, so UNVERIFIED). https://jobscan.co/blog/jobscan-vs-teal/
- Honesty: help text reportedly tells users to add only keywords that reflect real experience (example: do not claim Salesforce) `[snippet]`.
- Students: no student-specific offer found.

**Rezi**
- Positioning: ATS-focused resume builder with "AI Keyword Targeting".
- Score: "Rezi Score" 1-100 from five categories (Content, Format, Optimization, Best Practices, Application Ready), no weights given; Optimization depends on keywords from the pasted ad. States "real ATS systems don't give your resume an official score" and "Recruiters won't see the same percentage you see in Rezi". https://www.rezi.ai/rezi-docs/the-rezi-score-explained
- Keywords: marks missing terms and can generate a bullet containing each missing keyword for the user to accept or edit. Advice: "Avoid stuffing keywords into every sentence" and "completely inventing skills? You're entering risky territory." https://www.rezi.ai/rezi-docs/ai-keyword-targeting-explained
- Price: Free $0 (1 resume; Score and targeting "Limited"), Pro $29/month, Lifetime $149. https://www.rezi.ai/pricing
- Students: no student-specific offer verified.

**Enhancv**
- Positioning: resume builder with a free checker; Norwegian-language pages exist.
- Score: 27 checks in seven categories (ATS essentials, sections, content, job tailoring, red flags, bias, seniority); score combines parse rate and issues found; page says no tool gives "a true ATS score". Pasting an ad lists skills and keywords present and missing. https://enhancv.com/resume-checker/
- Price: checker free; builder free plan is a 7-day trial with branding; no student discount listed; Pro price not readable on the page. https://enhancv.com/pricing/
- Honesty: lists "Don't lie on your resume" among mistakes but describes no check for it; says to work keywords in "in a natural manner". https://enhancv.com/resume-checker/
- Norwegian cover-letter generator: free, no account, CV upload (PDF/DOCX, 2 MB) plus pasted ad; claims it never shares data or uses it to train AI (self-declared). https://enhancv.com/nb-no/soknadsbrev-generator/

**Kickresume**
- Positioning: AI resume and cover-letter builder ("GPT-4.1 powered cover letter writer"); output called a "first draft". https://www.kickresume.com/en/help-center/alternative-to-enhancv/
- Price: Free (templates; AI writer and AI Resume Checker not included), Monthly EUR 24, Quarterly EUR 18/month, Yearly EUR 8/month. https://www.kickresume.com/pricing
- Students: ISIC/ITIC/UNiDAYS-verified students get free Premium (help center says 6 months). https://www.kickresume.com/pricing ; https://www.kickresume.com/en/help-center/alternative-to-enhancv/
- Score and honesty: pages do not explain how the checker scores and make no accuracy or fabrication statement.

**Careerflow**
- Positioning: job-search suite (resume builder, LinkedIn optimizer, tracker, cover letters); special pricing for students and recent graduates and university partnerships. https://www.careerflow.ai/faq
- Score: its "Resume Analysis score" measures completion of resume sections (6 must-have, 5 nice-to-have), not job fit; a separate Resume Skill Score compares skills with a job description. https://help.careerflow.ai/en/articles/10008189-understanding-the-resume-analysis-score
- Price: free tier with limited AI; premium prices UNVERIFIED. Honesty: no statement on AI accuracy or fabrication on the FAQ.

### A3. Gap for a student-first, honest, Norwegian tool
- Language/market: only Enhancv produces Norwegian output among the tools checked, and only as a generic generator with no explained score; the Norwegian-market offerings found are guidance pages, courses, human career centres or an unverified app.
- Students: only Kickresume and Careerflow advertise student offers; none of the pages read describes mapping coursework or projects to ad keywords. Human guidance recognises the need (NAV: list courses and skills; NTNU: describe relevant education in 2-3 sentences, list project theme) but nothing automates it.
- Transparency: Jobscan, Rezi and Resume Worded disclose categories or a priority order but no weights or formula; Careerflow's score measures completeness, not fit. A published deterministic formula with per-keyword evidence is not offered by any of them according to their pages.
- Honesty: warnings sit in help text (Jobscan, Rezi, Teal, NTNU) while Rezi's flow generates a bullet for each missing keyword, so "evidence from the CV first" is not enforced by the product.
- Price: paid tiers run about $13/week to $49.95/month (Teal+, Rezi $29, Jobscan $49.95, Kickresume EUR 8-24/month); student relief only at Kickresume (6 months) and Careerflow.
- Market signal: cover letters are losing weight at some Norwegian employers (FINN, Skatteetaten) while NAV and NTNU still teach a tailored one-page letter; FINN says "man må faktisk tilpasse og skreddersy CV-en". https://www.finn.no/bedriftskunde/aktuelt/jobbindeks/soknadsbrevet-er-dodt
- Caveat: based on vendor pages and docs, not hands-on trials of the products.

### B2. What the systems do with applications
- Webcruiter's "automatisk grovsortering" works from screening questions that cover the ad's "absolutte krav" (must-haves); the page does not mention CV keyword matching or AI scoring. https://talentech.com/no/losninger/webcruiter/screening-og-automatisk-grovsortering/
- Talentech's blog: applicants who fail a must-have "går ikke videre i prosessen"; qualified ones can be "rangert automatisk ut fra resultatene" of the questions; recruiters then pass on the most interesting. Keyword filtering and AI are not mentioned. https://blog.talentech.com/no/rekrutter-raskere-med-effektiv-screening
- Talentech's newer ATS AI screening gives each application a 1-10 "text-to-text match" between ad/requirements and application documents plus screening answers; the vendor says it is "to assist and inspire, not to make decisions", that a match "won't always be right", and that recruiters should evaluate the whole application. The page does not name the product. https://talentech.com/select-the-right-candidate-faster-fairer-and-more-effective/
- Teamtailor "Job match score": per a search summary of its support article it is computed from recruiter-entered, weighted 1-5 ratings of scorecard skills, not from CV text `[snippet]` https://support.teamtailor.com/en/articles/7891886-job-match-score . A third-party guide claims Teamtailor has no screening score (conflict, UNVERIFIED). https://www.jobmentis.com/en/guide/teamtailor-ats
- No independent Norwegian survey of how many employers auto-rank or auto-filter was found. A PwC Norway AI-in-HR survey exists but its PDF was unreadable (UNVERIFIED). https://www.pwc.no/no/publikasjoner/kunstig-intelligens-i-hr.pdf
- Norwegian recruiters quoted by FINN stress individual assessment (HR Norge: do not "la teknologien overta hele prosessen") and note recruitment AI is high-risk under the EU AI Act. https://www.finn.no/bedriftskunde/aktuelt/jobbindeks/rekruttererens-ai-dilemma

### B3. "ATS rejects your CV": myth vs reality
- Against automatic rejection (US, vendor-run): Enhancv interviewed 25 US recruiters (Sep-Oct 2025); 23 (92%) said their ATS does not auto-reject for formatting, content or design, 2 (8%) auto-reject beyond knockout questions; 44% have an AI match score, 36% use it only as a guide, 8% use it to decide; recruiters review manually (400-600 applicants for entry-level roles); knockout questions are the usual hard filter. Caveats: n=25, self-reported, sold by a resume-tool vendor, its own figures are not fully consistent. https://enhancv.com/blog/does-ats-reject-resumes/
- Keyword screens do exclude people: HBS/Accenture "Hidden Workers" (UK, US, Germany) found automated screens filter out qualified candidates (e.g. employment-gap rules, keyword mismatch); co-chair Joseph Fuller: "If they're not there, you don't get considered"; 27 million hidden workers in the US. https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2021/09/new-study-says-hidden-workers-are-being-excluded/ . US case Mobley v. Workday (AI screening and age bias) is ongoing; a collective was preliminarily certified in May 2025 `[snippet]`. https://clearinghouse.net/case/44074/
- Vendors disagree: Rezi says real ATS give no official score yet "many systems rank or filter resumes based on keyword relevance" and recruiters "may also search databases using specific terms". https://www.rezi.ai/rezi-docs/the-rezi-score-explained . Jobscan states 70% are rejected without a source; the widely repeated "75% rejected" figure is traced to a 2012 marketing claim with no methodology `[snippet]`. https://itbrief.co.uk/story/study-reveals-ats-rarely-auto-rejects-cvs-debunks-75-myth
- Norwegian commentary: CV-service vendor Jobseeker.com says ATS gives each CV a "treffprosent" and unmatched CVs are "sortert bort automatisk", citing its own unsourced statistics; no independent Norwegian measurement exists in what I found, so Norwegian prevalence of keyword auto-filtering is UNVERIFIED. https://www.jobseeker.com/no/cv/artikler/rekrutteringssystem-ats-cv
- Regulation pushes toward human review: EURES (27 Aug 2026) says no AI tool may make final hiring decisions without a qualified person and jobseekers can request human review. https://eures.europa.eu/jobseekers-know-your-rights-age-ai-hiring-2026-08-27_no
- Honest framing (inference): the evidence supports describing the score as a proxy for (a) keyword search/ranking in recruiter tools and (b) how fast a human skimmer finds the required terms; it does not support claims about auto-rejection or interview odds. The only automatic gate documented for Norwegian ATS is must-have screening questions, which a keyword score does not model.

## C. Keyword matching in Norwegian

### C1. Known challenges
- Compounds: Norwegian compounds are normally written as one word; split spelling ("særskriving") is a common error and spell-checkers rarely catch it; hyphens appear in some loanword/abbreviation compounds (e.g. "fair play-normer"). So "prosjekt ledelse" in a CV or ad is plausible and should match "prosjektledelse" (inference). https://sprakradet.no/godt-og-korrekt-sprak/rettskriving-og-grammatikk/eitt-eller-fleire-ord/ `[snippet]`
  - A paraphrase such as "ledelse av prosjekter" is a synonym/phrase problem, not a spelling variant (inference).
- Decompounding trade-off: for Danish/Scandinavian query expansion, splitting compounds finds synonymous phrases but "the number of irrelevant hits is generally very high" unless constrained by noun-phrase analysis. https://aclanthology.org/L06-1350/ . Norwegian compound splitting has been studied since the 1980s (Fjeldvig and Golden, title only verified) https://aclanthology.org/W85-0107/ `[snippet]`; Elasticsearch's dictionary decompounder is "built for Germanic languages". https://www.elastic.co/guide/en/elasticsearch/reference/current/analysis-dict-decomp-tokenfilter.html
- Inflection: definite and plural forms (the brief's example: prosjektleder / prosjektlederen / prosjektledere). Stemmers strip suffixes but over-stem: Snowball's Norwegian sample output reduces "havneloven" to "havn" and "havneeiere" to "havneei", and "havnefylkene" to "havnefylk" (no compound splitting). https://snowballstem.org/algorithms/norwegian/stemmer.html . Exact-match tools miss morphology (Jobscan example in A1).
- Two written standards: Bokmål and Nynorsk. Snowball and Lucene's minimal stemmer cover both; Norsk Ordbank has separate editions. State bodies and county municipalities (and universities' administrative work) are under quota rules for written Nynorsk/Bokmål (Språklova §§ 13-14), so public-sector ads may contain Nynorsk; whether job ads fall under the quota is UNVERIFIED. https://www.regjeringen.no/no/tema/kultur-idrett-og-frivillighet/sprak-og-litteratur/innsiktsartikler/bruk-av-norsk-i-offentlege-organ/id2894906/
- Synonyms and paraphrase: suffix stemming cannot link "utvikler"/"programmerer" or "ledelse av prosjekter"/"prosjektledelse" (inference). Multilingual skill-extraction research links ad phrases to ESCO, and ESCO's own multilingual model was benchmarked on Norwegian ads `[snippet]`. https://aclanthology.org/2025.genaik-1.15 ; https://esco.ec.europa.eu/en/use-esco
- English mixed into Norwegian: English words enter most easily in technology, marketing and finance (Riksmålsforbundet grammar) `[snippet]` https://www.riksmalsforbundet.no/grammatikk/kapittel-16-ordvalg-og-stil/2 . Expect forms like "Python-erfaring", "Scrum-master", "agile/agil" and Norwegian endings on English roots; keep an English-Norwegian alias list and treat tool names as exact tokens (inference).
- Function words: Snowball ships a bokmål stopword list of about 170 words (Nynorsk-only words marked); ads also need domain boilerplate filtered (inference). https://snowballstem.org/algorithms/norwegian/stop.txt
- Validation gap: I found no published evaluation of Norwegian stemmers or lemmatizers for CV-to-ad matching; a 2006 NTNU thesis reported about 16% precision gain from a Norwegian analyzer (stopwords + stemming) in DotLucene `[snippet]`. https://ntnuopen.ntnu.no/ntnu-xmlui/handle/11250/251478

### C2. Open resources (names only, pointers for architecture)
- Stemmer, stopwords: Snowball Norwegian stemmer and stopword list https://snowballstem.org/algorithms/norwegian/stemmer.html ; Apache Lucene Norwegian analysis (NorwegianAnalyzer, NorwegianLightStemFilter, NorwegianMinimalStemFilter, NorwegianNormalizationFilter) https://lucene.apache.org/core/9_9_0/analysis/common/org/apache/lucene/analysis/no/package-summary.html

- Models and embeddings: NB-BERT (NbAiLab, CC BY 4.0) https://huggingface.co/NbAiLab/nb-bert-base ; NorBERT 3 (LTG) https://huggingface.co/ltg/norbert3-base `[snippet]`; NorBench benchmark incl. lemmatization https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.03880 `[snippet]`

## D. Privacy and GDPR for CVs sent to third-party LLM APIs

### D1. CVs as personal data
- Personal data is anything linkable to a person directly or indirectly; Datatilsynet lists name, e-mail, national ID number, recognisable photos and indirect identifiers. https://www.datatilsynet.no/rettigheter-og-plikter/personopplysninger/
- Ten special categories (ethnicity, politics, religion, philosophical belief, union membership, genetic, biometric ID, health, sex life, sexual orientation) are prohibited by default and need a separate basis such as explicit consent on top of the normal one. CVs can contain such data, e.g. union roles or health notes (inference). https://www.datatilsynet.no/rettigheter-og-plikter/virksomhetenes-plikter/om-behandlingsgrunnlag/spesielt-om-sarlige-kategorier-av-personopplysninger/
- Fødselsnummer may be used only where there is an objective need for secure identification; a CV tool has none. https://www.datatilsynet.no/rettigheter-og-plikter/personopplysninger/fodselsnummer/

### D2. Roles, legal basis, DPA, DPIA
- The app operator is the controller and the LLM provider a processor (Anthropic's DPA: "Customer is the controller and Anthropic is Customer's processor"). https://www.anthropic.com/legal/data-processing-addendum

## Implications for the PRD

1. **Call the score a vocabulary-coverage proxy, not an ATS score or an outcome predictor.** No independent Norwegian data shows auto-filtering (B3); the only automatic gate documented for Norwegian ATS is must-have screening questions (B2); AI text-match scores are recruiter aids with humans deciding (B2); even Rezi and Enhancv say no official ATS score exists. Do not carry the brief's "filtered out before a human reads it" or the addendum's unsourced 49%/62% figures into the PRD as facts; phrase the risk as being missed by keyword search and skim-reading, and keep unsourced percentages out of UI copy.
2. **Make the explanation the differentiator and spec it concretely.** Competitors publish only categories or a priority order (A1, A3). Require an in-app formula description and, per keyword, required vs preferred weight, match type (exact, inflection, compound, alias) and the CV location that supplies the evidence; same input, same score (SC2).
3. **Model hard requirements separately from keyword coverage.** Degree level, licences, language and work permit are what knockout questions test (B2) and a keyword score cannot capture them. Surface them as "krav du må oppfylle" in the gap analysis and never suggest wording that implies meeting one.
4. **Turn honesty into enforceable rules, not help text.** Incumbent warnings live in documentation while Rezi's flow still generates a bullet per missing keyword (A1, A3). Keep SC3, SC5 and SC6; add: a missing skill produces a gap with a how-to-acquire note, never insertable text; no one-click "add keyword"; every phrasing suggestion cites its CV entry; the manual AI-text checklist (feedback F21) includes NTNU's cautions (AI repeats the ad's text; the student must defend everything at interview).
5. **Position the letter as a short, example-driven draft and give CV tailoring equal weight; support a CV-only path.** NAV and NTNU still teach a tailored one-page letter, but FINN and Skatteetaten say its value is falling and 86% of recruiters notice AI use while 69% find applications more impersonal (A2). Letters should cite concrete CV or project examples, avoid repeating the CV, and be labelled as a draft.
6. **Define Norwegian matching as a pipeline with named match types and negative tests.** Normalise (case, æøå, hyphen/space) then lemma or stem, then compound handling restricted to ad keywords (avoids the noise in C1), then a curated English-Norwegian and abbreviation alias list seeded from ESCO labels (C2). Show which stage matched. Fixtures need negative cases (over-stemming such as havn*, "ledelse" vs "prosjektledelse"). Pin library versions for determinism. v1 is bokmål, but expect Nynorsk in public-sector ads.
7. **Write LLM-provider requirements as NFRs before the architecture picks a vendor.** Paid or commercial API tier only; written DPA with sub-processor list; contractual no-training; documented retention (OpenAI and Anthropic both state 30 days by default); EEA-resident processing, or a DPF-listed provider with an SCC fallback and a written contingency plan (D3). Reject free tiers (Gemini's unpaid tier forbids personal data and EEA apps must use paid; Mistral Studio free may train) (D4).
8. **Minimise and expire data by design.** Mask name, contact details, address, birth date, photo and ID numbers before any LLM call and re-insert locally; send only the sections needed; avoid logging prompts and responses. Pair encrypted storage (S7) with a defined retention period and a delete-my-data path (target deletion within one month of account closure, per Datatilsynet's digital-services guidance). Tell users not to include special-category data. The Norwegian privacy notice (C10) should name the provider, processing location and retention.
9. **Settle student-project compliance before real users touch the app.** Check the institution's student-project rules (UiA and NHH show they exist), keep fictional CVs for dev and test (already in the brief), and write a short DPIA-style risk note. List the formal DPIA need and AI Act applicability as open questions. Label letters "utkast skrevet med KI".
10. **Position as free, honest, Norwegian and student-first, with humans as the complement.** The verified gap (A3): no Norwegian tool found pairs an explained score with letter and gap analysis; only Kickresume and Careerflow target students; incumbents cost about $13-50/month. Do not claim a technical moat (the brief already concedes this). Pointing users to university career centres for a human CV check (UiO, NTNU, NHH) fits without making them part of v1.
